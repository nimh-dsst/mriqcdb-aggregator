An upload is one submitted report. The same image can be processed or submitted repeatedly. A scan in a deduplicated view is one admitted identity group under the policy below. It is not a unique person: different software versions or relevant settings can keep the same image in separate groups.

How deduplication works

The policy first groups uploads by an exact identity key, then tests the whole group. Matching one neighbour is not enough: a chain of similar uploads cannot conceal a large difference between its endpoints. Raw uploads are retained.

BOLD: K4+

The key combines the image-content MD5, the original MRIQC version string, the framewise displacement threshold and the normalized motion-correction method (AFNI, FSL or unknown). Every value in the 36-metric vector must be finite. Five count-related metrics must agree exactly; each of the 31 continuous metrics must have a whole-group range no larger than one millionth (10⁻⁶) of its frozen reference interquartile range (IQR). This admits tiny numerical differences, not substantial processing variation.

T1-weighted and T2-weighted: K3++

The key combines image-content MD5, the exact MRIQC version string and its testing setting. Image dimensions and voxel spacing must agree exactly, and all 62 compared metrics must be finite. The largest metric range, divided by that metric’s reference IQR, must be at most 0.1. This allows more run-to-run variation than K4+, including small differences in structural segmentation. The reference scales are frozen per policy version; a metric with zero reference IQR uses its non-zero-value IQR.

One observed representative

An admitted group contributes a medoid: an actual observed metric vector with the smallest total absolute distance to the other distinct vectors after IQR scaling. Repeated copies of a vector do not give it extra votes. Ties use the lowest upload ID. This preserves a real report instead of inventing a scan by averaging its metrics.

Why unstable groups are left out

A group is unstable when required values are missing or non-finite, exact fields disagree, or its diameter exceeds the policy tolerance. The deduplicated view leaves out the whole group because one representative would hide that disagreement. “Unstable” describes the evidence for merging, not a clinical assessment of image quality. The raw and combined views retain these uploads. Content hashes also cannot merge the same acquisition saved into different files; this limitation is particularly relevant to T2-weighted data.

Run-to-run stability

canonical_diameter is the largest within-group metric range divided by its frozen reference IQR. It measures disagreement between repeat runs of the same keyed input, not distance from the medoid, and it is not a percentage or a quality score. Zero means the compared continuous metrics agree; smaller values mean more stable results.

Choose a deduplicated view, open More filters and set the maximum Run-to-run stability to, for example, 0.01 for a stricter structural subset. Leave the minimum empty to include zero. The default policy still admits up to 0.1 for structural scans and 10⁻⁶ for BOLD. Unstable raw uploads in the combined view have no canonical diameter, so applying this range excludes them.

Three views per modality

  • Every upload (raw) counts all submissions. Repeated uploads of one image contribute repeatedly.
  • Deduplicated, the default, counts one representative per admitted group: K4+ for BOLD and K3++ for T1-weighted and T2-weighted images.
  • Deduplicated + unstable uploads adds every raw upload from excluded groups to the representatives. This mixes scans and uploads, not one scan per group.

Filters and chart counts use the chosen view. A metric’s count includes only finite values of that metric, so it can be smaller than the population total.

Comparisons and KS distance

A cohort is a named selection with its own view, filters and optional brushed range. Comparisons normalize each cohort by its own count. Histogram overlays use identical bin edges over a shared range. Density curves smooth those histograms with a Gaussian kernel at Silverman’s bandwidth, floored at 1.5 bin widths; they are estimates at histogram resolution. Values outside the displayed range are reported separately.

The differences table uses the selected reference cohort. Median shifts in IQR units use the reference’s IQR. The approximate Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) distance is the largest gap between the two cumulative histogram shares on their shared grid, including each cohort’s below-range mass. Zero means no detected distributional difference at that resolution; values nearer one mean stronger separation. It is an effect-size distance, not a p-value. The ≈ sign matters because the largest true gap may lie inside a bin. Cohorts can overlap, so their counts should not be added as if they were distinct scans.

For what each measurement means, see the MRIQC image-quality metric documentation.

Charts show at most six named series plus grey Other. On comparisons, Other pools the remaining cohort memberships, so overlapping scans contribute once per member cohort. Its quantiles are estimates from shared histogram bins; quantiles outside that grid are left unavailable. On categorical splits, Other is the remaining groups.
